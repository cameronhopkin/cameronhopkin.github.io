On October 7 I took twelve pictures of my kitchen with my phone. The freezer. The fridge door and the part of the inside I could see past it. The pantry shelves. The counter.

By that night I had a sorted inventory, seven family dinners for October 11 through 17, and a grocery list split between Sam's Club and Publix, priced at $188.88 against a $200 week.

Pictures of food took most of a 30-hour job off my plate.

That number is mine. Nobody ran a stopwatch. We have a lot of food in this house. Done the old way, the whole job runs 30 hours or more: going through everything we already have and writing it down, building meal plans around it, turning that into a grocery list, checking prices across two stores, redoing it when the total runs over, and then the shopping. The bots took everything but the shopping, and even that goes faster with a list already sorted by store. I have done the job the old way. I know how long I let it take.

In the last post I said I was pointing a small set of AI agents at my life, not just at drafting posts. This is the first report on what they have actually done, including where they got it wrong.

## The Setup

There are five lanes: faith, health, work, threat intel, and this blog. One coordinator, Grok Bot, routes work between them and keeps a shared brief on who I am, what I have committed to, and the lines nobody crosses.

Claude sits outside that team on purpose. Claude holds my history going back more than a year, and its job is to check the others. When a bot drifts, invents something, or gets a fact about me wrong, Claude is supposed to catch it and say so out loud.

Everything lands in a private hub that runs on my own machine. It is not on the internet and it never will be. It is where the bots report. It does not get the first hour of my day.

The whole team is on a one-month trial that started October 7. Every week each bot has to pass a check and show it is worth about thirty dollars a month to me. If it is not, it stops. I set that rule because I have a history of buying tools before I have anything to feed them. A bot that cannot earn thirty dollars of real use is a subscription with a personality.

## The Pantry

The health lane took the twelve photos and sorted everything it could read into freezer, fridge, pantry, and counter. Every item got checked against my food rules: high protein, low carb and sugar, and a short list of foods I never eat. Things I should skip got marked family-only instead of disappearing. Anything it could not read in a photo got marked "check" instead of guessed at. That one detail earned more of my trust than the rest of it.

Then the dinners. Seven nights, sized for three or four people, in the styles my family already cooks: Puerto Rican, Mexican, Mediterranean, Spanish. Pollo guisado on Sunday. Albondigas on Friday. As much as possible comes out of what was already sitting in the freezer and the pantry. Every dinner makes enough for my lunch the next day, and the protein target is built into the plate instead of bolted on afterward.

Then the list. Sam's Club came to $86.40 and Publix to $102.48. The prices came off the live store sites and that week's Publix ad. Anything sold by weight is labeled as an estimate. There is a cut-first list in case the cart runs over. Asparagus was $7.75 a bunch, so it got swapped for green beans and romaine.

All of it went to the hub as checklists I can work from my phone in the aisle.

## What It Got Wrong

The first pass had to be redone, and I want that on the page right next to the good part.

It planned around eggs and sausage. Day after day of eggs and sausage. My house does not eat that way. My wife and daughter do most of the cooking, and the first plan ignored the kitchen they actually run.

The grocery total did not add up either. The items came to about $111 and the list said $122. A list that cannot add cannot be trusted anywhere else. Claude caught both of those, which is exactly the job I gave it. I sent the corrections. Cook what the family cooks. Size it for three or four. Recheck the asparagus. Put the list where I can open it. The second version is the one above, and it is better because somebody pushed back. These things draft. They do not decide.

The faith lane had its own miss. It was set to deliver the morning Scripture at a time that would have put a screen in my hand right after five, and my rule is prayer and the Word before any screen. Now it posts the night before, so it is waiting for me instead of pulling at me. Psalm 5:3 (NKJV): "My voice You shall hear in the morning, O LORD; In the morning I will direct it to You, And I will look up." The first voice of my morning should not be a notification.

## What Else Shipped

The threat intel lane put together a brief on October 7. Every item carried a source link, and there was a section listing what it could not confirm. One item was timely enough that I passed the brief along. That counts toward the thirty dollars.

The reboot went up on October 7 too. A bot helped draft it, Claude went through it for my voice, I signed off, and it went up. The line on this site still holds: written by me, processed through Claude.

## Who Gets the Hours

Thirty hours is a lot of Saturdays. The question I answer for is where they go.

James 4 has been sitting on me since the men's study. Verse 14 (NKJV): "you do not know what will happen tomorrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapor that appears for a little time and then vanishes away." If my life is a vapor, I cannot spend saved hours building a bigger machine to save more hours. They go back to the early window with the Word. To breakfast with my wife. To the mat with my daughter. To the third lift.

The bots do not get the thinking, the obedience, or the counsel. Counsel comes from my wife first and then the men on Tuesday night. The bots draft and check. If the hub turns into a place I go to feel productive instead of a way to finish the ordinary work, that goes on this page too.

Verse 15 is how I am holding the trial: "If the Lord wills, we shall live and do this or that." One month. Thirty dollars of real use per bot, every week, or it goes. If the Lord wills, I will tell you which ones made it.

*Twelve pictures of a kitchen. Now I answer for the hours they gave back.*
